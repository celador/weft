Questions

What is commercial use?

Using Weft on code you are paid for: your employer's code, a client's code, or a product that earns money, your own included. Personal projects, learning, unpaid open-source work and 30 days of trying Weft at work are free. The full terms are on the license page.

Do I need a license if I have Pro?

No. Pro includes the commercial license for every Pro user, for as long as the subscription runs.

Yearly or lifetime license?

Both cover the same thing. Yearly is $29 per user and renews each year; if you cancel, it covers you to the end of the year you paid for. Lifetime is $58 per user, paid once, and never renews.

How does Weft check my license?

It doesn't. Weft never blocks anything and never reports how you use it. npx @celador/weft license shows what your account holds. On a machine that never signs in, record the license key from your account page with npx @celador/weft license <key>.

What counts as a user?

One person whose coding agents Weft coordinates. Agents don't count: one person running five agents is one user, and a team of four needs four. You pick the number at checkout. Pro users can change it any time under Manage billing on your account; to cover more people with a license, buy more users there.

Can teammates on other machines join my space?

Yes. Run npx @celador/weft invite in your repo. It prints a one-time code and the command your teammate runs in their clone. They get their own key, never yours, and their agents' edits are checked against yours. npx @celador/weft members shows who joined and removes anyone at once. Pro is per user: add a seat for each person on your account page. Each of your own machines connects with npx @celador/weft login.

What happens when a free hosted space passes 5,000 events?

Your agents keep working, but their edits aren't coordinated until the next month (UTC), and each agent is told so. Reads and the feed keep working. Upgrade to Pro, or switch to your own gateway with npx @celador/weft use local, which has no limit. An event is one edit, claim, release or checkpoint an agent adds to the log. Heartbeats, reads, checks that pass and everything a human does are free.

Where does my code go?

On Free and with a commercial license it stays on your machine; self-hosted and Pilot send edit events to a Worker in your Cloudflare account. On Pro, edit events (file paths, diffs, symbol names) go to Weft's gateway on Cloudflare and stay there until you erase the space with npx @celador/weft uninstall --space. Clerk holds your sign-in (an emailed code; Weft has no passwords); Stripe holds your card and billing email; Weft's gateway keeps only your account id, the Stripe customer id, the plan, the number of users and your licenses. Privacy policy.

Does Weft stop agents from conflicting?

It catches conflicts while the agents are still editing: an agent whose edit relies on code another agent just changed gets a diagnostic in its tool result and fixes its own edit. You choose whether such an edit is blocked or only flagged (enforce or advise mode).

Can I cancel?

Yes, any time. Manage billing on your account (or npx @celador/weft billing) opens Stripe's billing page, where you change the number of users, update your card or cancel. When Pro ends, the space goes back to Free. Nothing is deleted. A cancelled yearly license covers you to the end of the year you paid for.

Who bills me?

Stripe, as "Atelier", in US dollars: Pro at the start of each month or year, a yearly license once a year, a lifetime license once.

Is it open source?

Yes. The source is AGPL-3.0 (the protocol and plugins are MIT) at github.com/celador/weft. The builds we distribute are free for personal use; work use needs a commercial license or Pro. Versions up to 0.3.0 stay MIT.

What if Weft goes down?

Agents keep working. After 5 seconds the adapter lets the edit through and tells the agent it was not coordinated.
